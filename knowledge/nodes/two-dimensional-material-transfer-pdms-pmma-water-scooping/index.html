Core idea: The three routes solve the same adhesion-sequencing problem with different mechanical supports and landing modes. Direct PDMS and PMMA-plus-handle routes keep the film on a solid handle during placement; water-surface scooping deliberately creates a free-floating PMMA/2D stack. In all three, cleanliness, one advancing interface, low bending and controlled release matter more than force.

Scope and Stack Orientation

This note covers the complete three-route family used in the supplied laboratory SOP for continuous two-dimensional films grown on sapphire:

Route Support during sapphire release Sapphire-release step Placement on target Final support removal
A. Direct PDMS approximately 200 µm PDMS sheet DI-water-assisted interfacial separation edge-first solid-stamp lamination slow thermal peel at 100 °C
B. PMMA + TRT/PDMS handle thin PMMA plus a removable rigid handle DI-water-assisted interfacial separation edge-first solid-handle lamination handle release at 90–100 °C, then PMMA dissolution
C. PMMA water scooping thin PMMA only shallow-angle release to the water surface target lifts the freely floating film from below PMMA dissolution after drying/bake

The laboratory shorthand “dry transfer” for Routes A and B refers to dry, handle-supported placement onto the receiver. It does not mean that the complete donor-to-receiver sequence is liquid-free: both routes use DI water to separate the 2D film from sapphire, and Route B later uses solvent to remove PMMA. Route C is distinct because the film is released as a freely floating membrane and is landed by scooping.

Before donor release, the relevant stacks are

\[\begin{aligned} \text{A: }&\text{air}|\text{PDMS}|\text{2D}|\text{sapphire},\\ \text{B: }&\text{air}|\text{TRT or PDMS}|\text{PMMA}|\text{2D}|\text{sapphire}. \end{aligned}\]

For Route C, the intended floating stack is

\[\text{air}\;|\;\text{PMMA}\;|\;\text{2D material}\;|\;\text{DI water},\]

so the PMMA remains above the 2D film and the newly exposed underside of the 2D material faces the water. During pickup, a target substrate approaches from below; the 2D material, not the PMMA, contacts the target.

None of the routes dissolves the donor as in metal-foil etching. Sapphire remains intact while water advances into the sapphire/2D interface; PDMS, or PMMA with or without a handle, supplies mechanical continuity. Release depends on growth history, interfacial contamination, edge access, film continuity, support thickness and residual stress.

Why Water Can Release the Film

Let \(S\), \(F\) and \(W\) denote sapphire, the PMMA-supported 2D film, and water. Replacing a unit area of the dry \(S/F\) interface with two water interfaces changes the interfacial free energy by

\[\frac{\Delta G_{\mathrm{int}}}{A} =\gamma_{S,W}+\gamma_{F,W}-\gamma_{S,F}.\]

Water invasion is thermodynamically favoured when this quantity is negative. Real delamination also pays bending, pinning and viscous-dissipation costs, so a practical propagation condition is better written as

\[G_{\mathrm{drive}} =\gamma_{S,F}-\gamma_{S,W}-\gamma_{F,W} >\Gamma_{\mathrm{pin}}+\Gamma_{\mathrm{bend}}+\Gamma_{\mathrm{diss}}.\]

The criterion explains several observations. A clean hydrophilic sapphire surface lowers the energy of the sapphire/water interface. A pre-existing edge opening gives water access. Particles or strongly bonded islands pin the crack front. Excessive forcing does not repair an unfavourable interface; it converts a release problem into a tear or crease.

PMMA is not merely a handle. Its flexural rigidity scales as

\[D=\frac{E_{\mathrm{PMMA}}h^3}{12(1-\nu^2)},\]

where \(h\) is PMMA thickness. A modest thickness increase can suppress local folding because \(D\propto h^3\), but also raises bending stress, solvent-removal time and residue burden. The useful coating is therefore the thinnest continuous film that survives release and pickup for the chosen sample size.

The Two Moving Fronts

1. Release front: sapphire to water surface

The donor is introduced at a shallow angle so only one edge first meets water. The meniscus then forms a narrow water wedge and the delamination front travels across the sample. A whole-surface plunge creates several uncontrolled fronts, traps pockets, wets the PMMA top surface and can make the stack sink.

2. Pickup front: water surface to target substrate

The target is submerged below the floating stack and raised at a shallow angle. Contact should begin at one target edge and sweep across. Water drains toward the last open edge instead of being sealed into isolated blisters. The relevant speed measure is the capillary number

\[\mathrm{Ca}=\frac{\mu U}{\gamma},\]

with water viscosity \(\mu\), pickup velocity \(U\) and surface tension \(\gamma\). A low capillary number gives a quasi-static meniscus and is normally desirable. Sudden acceleration, rotation or reversal can launch waves and buckle a film even when the average speed seems slow.

The competition between gravity and surface tension grows with lateral size \(L\) according to

\[\mathrm{Bo}=\frac{\Delta\rho gL^2}{\gamma}.\]

Large unsupported films are therefore less forgiving than millimetre-scale pieces. A clean peripheral support frame can distribute surface-tension forces and is a defensible scale-up option; it is not required for the small-sample baseline described below.

Route A — Direct PDMS Transfer (Dry Placement)

Direct PDMS transfer minimizes organic processing on the active 2D surface and keeps the film mechanically registered throughout alignment. Its main limitation is mechanical: the approximately 200 µm PDMS sheet in the SOP is much thicker than the atomically thin film, so unintended stamp curvature can impose strain. For a support bent to radius \(R\), the outer-fibre strain scales as

\[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bend}}\simeq \frac{h_{\mathrm{PDMS}}}{2R},\]

which explains why doubling the support thickness or halving the bend radius can strongly increase cracking risk. “Soft” does not mean “strain-free”; the important operational variable is curvature.

  1. Donor preparation. Remove loose particles from the sapphire/2D surface with gentle, clean nitrogen. Remove both protective liners from the PDMS without touching the active face.
  2. PDMS lamination. Starting at one edge, laminate PDMS over the 2D film in one continuous pass. Maintain one advancing contact line and exclude bubbles. Do not repeatedly attach, lift and reattach, because every reversal creates a new strain and contamination history.
  3. Water-assisted donor separation. Introduce the stack nearly vertically. If it does not separate spontaneously, fully immerse it with PDMS downward and sapphire upward, then soak for approximately 5–10 min. Let water reach an open sapphire/2D edge. Use only gentle edge probing when necessary and never bow the PDMS to lever the film away.
  4. Retrieve and dry. Remove the separated PDMS/2D stack, rinse with clean DI water if required by the local contamination control, mechanically support the compliant PDMS and dry with a gentle nitrogen flow. Inspect for curvature, cracks, bubbles and missing regions before target alignment.
  5. Dry placement. Bring the PDMS/2D stack onto the prepared target from one edge to the other, again keeping a single contact front. Minimize bending and do not trap an air pocket by placing the stamp parallel to the target.
  6. Thermal release. Move rapidly to 70 °C, then ramp at approximately 10 °C min\(^{-1}\) to 100 °C. Hold at 100 °C according to the qualified local recipe and peel the PDMS slowly in one gradual, continuous direction. A pause-reverse-recontact peel can drag or delaminate the film.

This route is preferred for rapid alignment and stacking when the target tolerates the thermal cycle and avoiding PMMA residue is more important than maximizing tolerance to handle bending. Stop if the PDMS forms a tight radius, the release front becomes discontinuous, a crack propagates, or the film moves with the stamp during the 100 °C peel.

Route B — PMMA-Assisted Transfer with a TRT or PDMS Handle (Dry Placement)

Here a thin, conformal PMMA layer supports local film continuity while a thermal-release tape (TRT) or PDMS sheet provides a macroscopic handle. The composite separates local support from global handling: the thin layer reduces the strain imposed by a thick stamp, while the handle prevents a large PMMA/2D membrane from folding. For a laminated support, the effective bending rigidity contains both the layer self-rigidity and its distance \(z_i\) from the neutral axis,

\[D_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx \sum_i \frac{E_i}{1-\nu_i^2} \left(\frac{h_i^3}{12}+h_i z_i^2\right),\]

so handle thickness, modulus and lamination symmetry all matter; adding a handle is not mechanically neutral.

  1. PMMA support. Clean the donor with gentle nitrogen, spin PMMA at 4000 rpm and soft-bake at 110 °C, following the SOP starting window. Cool and inspect the coating for pinholes, edge recession and built-in curvature.
  2. Attach the handle. Laminate TRT or PDMS from one edge in a single bubble-free pass. Keep the handle outside the active film whenever possible and avoid repeated tack/de-tack cycles.
  3. Water-assisted separation. Introduce the stack nearly vertically. Allow spontaneous separation or soak for approximately 5–10 min; use gentle edge probing only when the water front is visibly pinned. Avoid bending the handle/PMMA laminate.
  4. Retrieve and dry. Mechanically support the released handle/PMMA/2D stack during DI-water rinse and gentle nitrogen drying. Do not align it to the receiver while visible water or gross curvature remains.
  5. Dry placement. Laminate onto the prepared target edge-first and bubble-free, using the handle for alignment while keeping the bending radius large.
  6. Release the handle at 90–100 °C. TRT should lose adhesion and lift away while PMMA/2D remains on the target; PDMS should be peeled gradually. Overheating TRT can deposit tape adhesive, whereas under-release or a fast peel can lift the PMMA/2D stack.
  7. Consolidate and strip PMMA. The SOP uses a 110 °C vacuum bake for 12 h, followed by acetone, IPA and gentle nitrogen drying. Treat these as validated starting conditions, not proof of a residue-free interface.

Choose this route for larger-area films and alignment-sensitive stacks when a conformal support is needed. Its penalties are a longer flow, a later wet solvent step, possible PMMA residue and a narrower thermal window when TRT is used. Record the handle material and lot, pressure, dwell, release temperature, peel angle and any visible adhesive residue.

Use the route that protects the dominant yield limiter. If cracking follows PDMS curvature, move from A to B. If handle release or tape residue dominates, use a PDMS handle, requalify the thermal window, or consider C. If C folds or traps water, improve the contact-line control or move to a handle-supported route. A method name is not a cleanliness guarantee: verify each route by the same optical, spectroscopic, topographic and electrical metrics.

Route C — PMMA Water-Surface Scooping (Wet Placement)

Route C deliberately removes the rigid handle. The first controlled front releases PMMA/2D from sapphire to the water surface; the second lands it onto the target while preserving one drainage path.

A. Prepare the water surface and tools

  1. Use a clean glass or otherwise compatible vessel with enough depth to place the target below the floating film without touching the bottom. Rinse the vessel and tweezers according to the laboratory cleanliness protocol.
  2. Fill with fresh DI water. Treat the air/water surface as a process interface: visible particles, solvent vapour condensate, dust and residues from a reused vessel can all be transferred to the 2D material.
  3. Arrange illumination so the floating PMMA outline, release front and target edges are visible. Eliminate airflow, vibration and nearby rapid motion before release.
  4. Confirm that the PMMA is continuous beyond the 2D film perimeter. A notch, pinhole or uncoated edge is a likely tear nucleation site.

B. Establish the PMMA support

The supplied laboratory SOP uses a 4000 rpm PMMA spin followed by a 110 °C soft bake. These values are a starting recipe, not a universal specification: PMMA molecular weight, concentration, solvent, dispense volume, acceleration, humidity, sample size and topography all change the resulting thickness and stress.

After baking, cool the sample before water contact. Inspect for edge recession, bubbles, radial striations and cracks. Do not use a coating that is already discontinuous merely because its central region looks smooth.

C. Release onto the water surface

  1. Hold the sapphire by an edge, with the PMMA/2D side facing upward. Keep tweezers away from the active film area.
  2. Touch one donor edge to the DI-water surface at a shallow angle. For centimetre-scale samples, approximately \(5\)–\(15^\circ\) is a useful experimental starting window; the correct angle is set by sample size, edge geometry and support stiffness rather than by a universal number.
  3. Lower the donor gradually until the meniscus reaches an exposed sapphire/2D edge. Pause and watch for a coherent release front. Do not immerse the PMMA top surface abruptly.
  4. Let water propagate under the film. If the front stalls, wait first. If intervention is necessary, use a clean fine tool only at the existing edge opening to admit water; do not scrape beneath the active area or puncture the PMMA.
  5. Continue lowering, or raise the water level slowly, so the released region remains supported by surface tension. Avoid pulling the film upward with tweezers.
  6. When release is complete, move the sapphire away below or sideways from the floating film without crossing the air/water interface through the film.

Stop conditions: stop rather than force the process if the PMMA floods, the film begins to roll, more than one release front forms, a tear runs inward, or the sapphire remains strongly pinned after a clean edge has been exposed. Recover the stack if possible and diagnose the interface before repeating.

D. Scoop the floating film

  1. Place the cleaned target substrate in the bath below the floating PMMA/2D stack, active side upward. Approach from outside the film footprint so the substrate edge does not strike or drag it.
  2. Align the target under the film. For manual operation, align by moving the target, not by touching the floating PMMA with a tool.
  3. Tilt the target slightly and raise it until one target edge contacts the underside of the 2D film.
  4. Continue lifting in one slow, continuous motion. Maintain a single advancing contact line. Allow water to drain from the last, lowest edge; do not level the substrate until the film is fully supported.
  5. Hold the retrieved stack nearly stationary while the macroscopic water layer drains. Wick water only from a bare substrate edge with a compatible lint-free material; never touch the 2D-film area.
  6. Air-dry in a protected environment. Do not direct a high-velocity nitrogen jet across a wet, unsupported film: shear can wrinkle, move or tear it.

Target Preparation and SOP-Derived Starting Window

For a SiO\(_2\)/Si target, the supplied SOP specifies acetone ultrasonication for 5 min, IPA ultrasonication for 5 min, a thorough DI-water rinse, a 180 °C bake for 20 min, and O\(_2\) plasma at 30 sccm, 50 W for 5 min. The objective is a clean, wettable receiver that promotes uniform contact and drainage.

These conditions must be requalified for patterned devices, metals, organics, resists, suspended membranes and air-sensitive materials. Oxygen plasma can oxidize, charge, roughen or chemically modify a target; it should be applied to the bare compatible target before transfer, never assumed safe for an already integrated device.

After scooping, the supplied SOP calls for air drying, gentle removal of residual surface water, a 110 °C vacuum bake for 12 h, acetone dissolution of PMMA, an IPA rinse and gentle nitrogen drying. Record the ramp, pressure, solvent age and soak time. A long bake can improve adhesion and flatten the stack, but it can also change trapped contamination or polymer chemistry; it is not proof that the interface is clean.

Drying, Adhesion and Trapped Water

The visible film can look flat while a nanometre-to-micrometre water layer remains underneath. In a simplified lubrication picture, drainage time grows rapidly as the gap \(h\) closes,

\[t_{\mathrm{drain}}\sim\frac{\mu L^2}{\Delta p\,h^2},\]

where \(\Delta p\) is the pressure driving drainage. The expression is only a scaling law, but it explains why slow edge-first contact and staged drying outperform a rapid parallel landing followed by aggressive baking. Once the perimeter seals, heating may convert a continuous water layer into discrete blisters rather than remove it.

Use a staged approach: passive drainage first, then a compatible low-temperature bake, then the validated vacuum-bake schedule. Increase temperature only after optical inspection confirms that bubbles are not growing. Materials with volatile chalcogens, organic targets, pre-patterned polymers or thermally sensitive contacts require a separate thermal budget.

Acceptance Criteria and Process Records

A transfer should be accepted by measured outcomes rather than by “film present”. Recommended records include:

  • optical maps before release and after transfer, with intact-area yield \(Y_A=A_{\mathrm{intact}}/A_{\mathrm{target}}\);
  • bubble, wrinkle, crack and missing-area fractions at a defined optical resolution;
  • Raman or photoluminescence peak position, linewidth and mapping statistics before and after transfer to separate strain, doping and damage;
  • AFM roughness and residue/particle density on representative regions;
  • electrical continuity, mobility, hysteresis or contact resistance when the transferred film is intended for devices;
  • route identifier; PDMS thickness/lot and minimum bend radius; PMMA grade, solution age and spin program; TRT/handle type and lot; lamination pressure and front direction; DI-water quality and bath age; separation time; placement angle/speed; release temperature, dwell and peel direction; target treatment, drying conditions and solvent lots.

Acetone removal of PMMA does not guarantee a polymer-free surface. Direct measurements have shown persistent residue and possible carbonization during some annealing treatments. Cleaning therefore needs a material-specific validation, not a ritual solvent sequence. Oxidizing cleaning that may work for graphene can damage semiconducting transition-metal dichalcogenides or contacts.

Safety and Method Boundaries

This node is a process guide, not a substitute for the laboratory risk assessment. Acetone and IPA are flammable solvents and belong in the approved fume-hood and waste stream. TRT and other pressure-sensitive tapes must be checked against the laboratory chemical and thermal limits; overheated adhesive can contaminate the sample and release vapour. Hot plates and heated substrates present burn and ignition hazards. Oxygen-plasma systems combine RF power, vacuum and reactive gas. Cleaved sapphire edges and scribing tools are sharp. Use pressure-regulated nitrogen and avoid directing gas toward people or unsupported films.

The method should be requalified whenever donor material, sapphire orientation, film size, PMMA formulation, target chemistry or device topography changes. Water-sensitive 2D materials, soluble targets and structures with open cavities may require a dry or encapsulated alternative.

Deep Derivation and Mechanistic Interpretation

The three routes share one interfacial inequality: water-driven crack propagation must overcome pinning, bending and dissipation. They then diverge mechanically. Route A must keep PDMS curvature low enough that \(h_{\mathrm{PDMS}}/(2R)\) remains below the film’s damage strain; Route B must balance composite support rigidity against handle-release shear and polymer residue; Route C must keep the pickup capillary number low enough to maintain a single drainage front. This framework explains why edge-first, low-acceleration motion is robust across all routes even though the exact angle, speed and thermal recipe remain tool-specific.

核心结论： 三条路线解决的是同一个“黏附顺序控制”问题，但机械支撑和落片方式不同：PDMS 直转与 PMMA+手柄路线在落片时始终由固体手柄承载；水面捞取则主动形成自由漂浮的 PMMA/二维材料层叠。三者都依赖洁净界面、单一推进前沿、低弯曲和受控释放，强行施力通常只会把难释放转化为裂纹或褶皱。

适用范围与层叠方向

本节点覆盖用户提供实验室 SOP 中从蓝宝石转移连续二维薄膜的完整三路线工艺族：

路线 蓝宝石释放时的支撑 供体释放 在目标上的落片方式 最终去除支撑
A. PDMS 直转 约 200 µm PDMS 薄片 去离子水辅助界面分离 固体印章单边先接触落片 100 °C 下缓慢热剥离
B. PMMA + TRT/PDMS 手柄 薄 PMMA 加可移除宏观手柄 去离子水辅助界面分离 固体手柄单边先接触落片 90–100 °C 释放手柄，再溶解 PMMA
C. PMMA 水面捞取 仅薄 PMMA 以浅角度释放到水面 目标衬底从下方捞取自由漂浮膜 干燥/烘烤后溶解 PMMA

实验室把 A、B 称作“干法”，准确含义是 在接收衬底上采用干式、手柄支撑的落片，而不是整个供体到接收体流程完全无液体：两者都用去离子水使二维材料脱离蓝宝石，B 还要用溶剂去除 PMMA。C 的本质区别，是薄膜脱离供体后成为自由漂浮膜，再由目标衬底捞取。

供体释放前，A、B 的层叠分别为

\[\begin{aligned} \text{A: }&\text{空气}|\text{PDMS}|\text{二维材料}|\text{蓝宝石},\\ \text{B: }&\text{空气}|\text{TRT 或 PDMS}|\text{PMMA}|\text{二维材料}|\text{蓝宝石}. \end{aligned}\]

C 路线理想的漂浮层叠为

\[\text{空气}\;|\;\text{PMMA}\;|\;\text{二维材料}\;|\;\text{去离子水},\]

即 PMMA 位于二维材料上方，新暴露出的二维材料背面朝向水。捞取时目标衬底从水下接近，因此最终接触目标衬底的是二维材料，而不是 PMMA。

三条路线都不同于刻蚀铜箔等金属生长衬底：蓝宝石保持完整，水从边缘进入蓝宝石/二维材料界面；PDMS、带或不带手柄的 PMMA 负责维持机械连续性。能否释放仍受生长历史、界面污染、边缘开放程度、薄膜连续性、支撑层厚度和残余应力共同控制。

水为什么能够释放薄膜

以 \(S\)、\(F\) 和 \(W\) 分别表示蓝宝石、PMMA 支撑的二维薄膜和水。单位面积干燥 \(S/F\) 界面被两个水界面取代时，自由能变化为

\[\frac{\Delta G_{\mathrm{int}}}{A} =\gamma_{S,W}+\gamma_{F,W}-\gamma_{S,F}.\]

当该量为负时，水进入界面在热力学上有利。真实剥离还需要克服弯曲、钉扎和黏性耗散，因此更实用的传播条件是

\[G_{\mathrm{drive}} =\gamma_{S,F}-\gamma_{S,W}-\gamma_{F,W} >\Gamma_{\mathrm{pin}}+\Gamma_{\mathrm{bend}}+\Gamma_{\mathrm{diss}}.\]

这个条件解释了常见现象：洁净且亲水的蓝宝石有利于形成蓝宝石/水界面；开放边缘为水提供入口；颗粒和强结合岛会钉扎剥离前沿；强行撬动并不能修复不利界面，只会把“难释放”转化成撕裂或折叠。

PMMA 不只是手柄，其弯曲刚度为

\[D=\frac{E_{\mathrm{PMMA}}h^3}{12(1-\nu^2)}.\]

由于 \(D\propto h^3\)，略微增加厚度就能明显抑制局部折叠；但过厚也会增加弯曲应力、去胶时间和残留负担。合适的 PMMA 应是在给定样品尺寸下能够经受释放和捞取的最薄连续支撑膜。

两条移动前沿

1. 释放前沿：从蓝宝石到水面

供体以浅角度入水，只让一条边先接触水。弯月面形成水楔，剥离前沿随后横向传播。整面快速压入水中会同时产生多个不可控前沿、夹入水囊、淹没 PMMA 上表面，并可能使整个层叠下沉。

2. 捞取前沿：从水面到目标衬底

目标衬底先潜入漂浮层叠下方，再以浅角度抬升。一侧边缘应最先接触，接触线随后扫过整个衬底，使水从最后一个开放边缘排出，而不是被封装成孤立气泡或水泡。速度可由毛细数描述：

\[\mathrm{Ca}=\frac{\mu U}{\gamma},\]

其中 \(μ\) 为水黏度，\(U\) 为抬升速度，\(γ\) 为表面张力。较低毛细数对应准静态弯月面，通常更稳健。即使平均速度很慢，突然加速、旋转或反向仍会激发水面波动并使薄膜屈曲。

重力与表面张力的竞争随横向尺度 \(L\) 增大，可用 Bond 数表示：

\[\mathrm{Bo}=\frac{\Delta\rho gL^2}{\gamma}.\]

因此，大面积无支撑薄膜比毫米级样品更难操作。洁净的外围支撑框可以分散表面张力，是合理的放大策略；但它不是本节点小样品起始流程的必要条件。

路线 A——PDMS 直转（干式落片）

PDMS 直转使二维材料在对准过程中始终保持机械定位，并减少活性表面对 PMMA 工艺的暴露。主要限制来自力学：SOP 中约 200 µm 厚的 PDMS 远厚于原子级薄膜，因此印章的非预期弯曲可能给二维材料施加应变。支撑层弯曲半径为 \(R\) 时，外表面应变约为

\[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bend}}\simeq \frac{h_{\mathrm{PDMS}}}{2R}.\]

这说明 PDMS 厚度加倍或弯曲半径减半，都可能明显增加裂纹风险。“软”并不等于“无应变”，真正需要控制的是曲率。

  1. 供体准备。 用洁净、柔和的氮气去除蓝宝石/二维材料表面的松散颗粒。移除 PDMS 两侧保护膜，不接触有效贴合面。
  2. 层压 PDMS。 从一侧边缘开始，用单次连续动作把 PDMS 覆盖到二维薄膜上，保持一条前进接触线并排除气泡。不能反复贴上、揭起再贴合，因为每次反向都会引入新的应变与污染历史。
  3. 水辅助供体分离。 使层叠接近竖直入水。若蓝宝石未自动脱离，则让 PDMS 朝下、蓝宝石朝上完全浸入，并浸泡约 5–10 min，使水到达开放的蓝宝石/二维材料边缘。只在必要时轻柔探触边缘，绝不能弯折 PDMS 充当撬杆。
  4. 取出与干燥。 取出 PDMS/二维材料层叠；若本地污染控制要求，可用洁净去离子水冲洗。吹氮时机械支撑柔软 PDMS，并检查曲率、裂纹、气泡和缺失区域，再进入对准。
  5. 干式落片。 让 PDMS/二维材料从一侧边缘开始贴到已准备的目标衬底上，始终保持单一接触前沿。尽量避免弯曲，不能平行压下后封住大面积空气。
  6. 热释放。 快速升至 70 °C，再以约 10 °C min\(^{-1}\) 升到 100 °C；按经验证配方保温，并沿单一方向缓慢、渐进、连续揭除 PDMS。暂停—反向—再次接触会拖动或揭起二维材料。

目标可承受该热循环、需要快速对准堆叠且希望避免 PMMA 残留时，优先考虑该路线。若 PDMS 形成很小弯曲半径、释放前沿断裂、裂纹持续扩展，或 100 °C 揭膜时二维材料随印章移动，应立即停止。

路线 B——PMMA 配合 TRT 或 PDMS 手柄（干式落片）

该路线用薄而共形的 PMMA 维持局部薄膜连续性，再用热释放胶带（TRT）或 PDMS 提供宏观手柄。薄层负责降低厚印章引起的局部应变，手柄则防止大面积 PMMA/二维材料自由膜折叠。层合支撑的等效弯曲刚度既包含各层自身刚度，也包含各层到中性轴的距离 \(z_i\)：

\[D_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx \sum_i \frac{E_i}{1-\nu_i^2} \left(\frac{h_i^3}{12}+h_i z_i^2\right).\]

因此，手柄厚度、模量和层压对称性都会影响应变；增加手柄并非力学上“无影响”。

  1. PMMA 支撑。 用柔和氮气清理供体，按 SOP 起始窗口以 4000 rpm 旋涂 PMMA，并在 110 °C 软烘；冷却后检查针孔、边缘退缩和内建翘曲。
  2. 贴附手柄。 TRT 或 PDMS 从一侧边缘开始，以单次、无气泡动作层压；手柄边缘尽量避开有效薄膜区，不能反复粘贴和揭开。
  3. 水辅助分离。 使层叠接近竖直入水，等待其自动分离，或浸泡约 5–10 min。只有在水前沿明显钉扎时才轻柔探触边缘，避免弯折手柄/PMMA 层合结构。
  4. 取出与干燥。 在去离子水冲洗和轻柔氮气吹干时，为释放出的手柄/PMMA/二维材料提供机械支撑；仍有可见水或明显翘曲时不要开始对准。
  5. 干式落片。 利用手柄进行对准，从单边开始、无气泡地贴到已准备的目标衬底上，并保持较大的弯曲半径。
  6. 在 90–100 °C 释放手柄。 TRT 应失黏并脱离，同时把 PMMA/二维材料留在目标上；PDMS 则应渐进揭除。TRT 过热可能留下胶黏剂，释放不足或揭除过快则可能把 PMMA/二维材料一并带走。
  7. 固着并去 PMMA。 SOP 采用 110 °C 真空烘烤 12 h，再依次使用丙酮、IPA 和柔和氮气。它们是需要复验的起始条件，不能作为“界面无残留”的证明。

对于大面积薄膜或对准敏感的层叠，当需要共形支撑时可优先选择该路线。代价是流程更长、后续仍有湿式溶剂步骤、可能出现 PMMA 残留，TRT 还具有较窄的热释放窗口。必须记录手柄材料与批次、压力、停留时间、释放温度、揭除角度及可见胶残留。

路线 C——PMMA 水面释放与捞取（湿式落片）

C 路线主动去掉刚性手柄：第一条受控前沿把 PMMA/二维材料从蓝宝石释放到水面；第二条前沿使其落到目标上，同时保留一条连续排水路径。

A. 准备水面与工具

  1. 使用洁净玻璃或其他相容容器，水深必须足以让目标衬底移动到漂浮膜下方而不碰底。按实验室洁净流程冲洗容器和镊子。
  2. 加入新鲜去离子水。把空气/水界面视为工艺界面：可见颗粒、溶剂蒸气凝结物、重复使用容器的残留和灰尘都会转移到二维材料上。
  3. 布置照明，使 PMMA 轮廓、剥离前沿和目标衬底边缘清晰可见；释放前消除气流、振动和附近的快速动作。
  4. 确认 PMMA 连续覆盖并越过二维薄膜边界。缺口、针孔或未覆盖边缘往往是撕裂起点。

B. 建立 PMMA 支撑

用户提供的实验室 SOP 采用 4000 rpm 旋涂 PMMA，随后进行 110 °C 软烘。这些参数是起始配方，不是普适标准；PMMA 分子量、浓度、溶剂、滴胶量、加速度、湿度、样品尺寸和表面起伏都会改变最终厚度与应力。

软烘后先冷却再接触水。检查边缘退缩、气泡、放射状条纹和裂缝。不能因为中心区域平整，就忽略已经不连续的支撑膜。

C. 释放到水面

  1. 只夹持蓝宝石边缘，使 PMMA/二维材料面朝上；镊子远离有效薄膜区域。
  2. 让供体的一条边以浅角度接触去离子水。对厘米级样品，约 \(5\)–\(15^\circ\) 可作为实验起始窗口；正确角度由样品尺寸、边缘形貌和支撑刚度决定，不是固定常数。
  3. 缓慢降低供体，直到弯月面接触开放的蓝宝石/二维材料边缘。停顿并观察是否形成连续释放前沿，不要突然淹没 PMMA 上表面。
  4. 让水自行向薄膜下方传播。前沿停滞时先等待；确需辅助时，只能用洁净细工具在现有边缘开口处帮助进水，不能刮擦有效区域下方或刺穿 PMMA。
  5. 继续缓慢降低供体，或缓慢升高水位，使已释放区域始终受到表面张力支撑；不要用镊子向上拉膜。
  6. 完全释放后，从水下或侧面移走蓝宝石，避免让蓝宝石穿过漂浮膜所在的空气/水界面。

停止条件： PMMA 上表面被淹没、薄膜开始卷曲、出现多个释放前沿、裂纹向内扩展，或开放洁净边缘后蓝宝石仍强烈钉扎时，应停止而不是强行推进。尽可能回收层叠，并在下一次尝试前诊断界面原因。

D. 捞取漂浮膜

  1. 将清洗后的目标衬底置于漂浮 PMMA/二维材料层叠 下方，有效面朝上。从膜的投影范围之外接近，避免衬底边缘撞击或拖拽薄膜。
  2. 在膜下对准目标衬底。手动操作时应移动目标衬底完成对准，而不是用工具触碰漂浮 PMMA。
  3. 让目标衬底略微倾斜并抬升，直到一侧边缘先接触二维材料背面。
  4. 继续以单次、缓慢、连续动作抬升，保持一条前进接触线，让水从最后且最低的一侧边缘排出；薄膜完全获得支撑前不要把衬底放平。
  5. 宏观水层排出时保持样品近似静止。若必须吸走水，只能在裸露衬底边缘使用相容无尘材料，绝不能接触二维材料区域。
  6. 在受保护环境中空气干燥。不要把高速氮气横向吹过仍湿润且缺乏支撑的薄膜，否则剪切力会导致起皱、位移或撕裂。

目标衬底准备与 SOP 起始窗口

对于 SiO\(_2\)/Si 目标衬底，用户提供的 SOP 规定：丙酮超声 5 min、IPA 超声 5 min、充分去离子水冲洗、180 °C 烘烤 20 min，以及 O\(_2\) 等离子体处理（30 sccm、50 W、5 min）。其目的是得到洁净、可润湿的接收表面，以促进均匀接触与排水。

图形化器件、金属、有机层、光刻胶、悬空结构和空气敏感材料必须重新验证这些条件。氧等离子体可能氧化、充电、粗化或化学改变目标表面；它只应作用于 转移前且确认相容的裸目标衬底，不能默认对已集成器件安全。

捞取后，原 SOP 采用空气干燥、温和去除残余表面水、110 °C 真空烘烤 12 h、丙酮溶解 PMMA、IPA 冲洗及轻柔氮气吹干。应记录升温过程、压力、溶剂新鲜度和浸泡时间。长时间烘烤可能改善黏附并使层叠变平，也可能改变被困污染物或聚合物化学；它不等于界面已经洁净。

干燥、黏附与夹水

肉眼看似平整时，界面仍可能存在纳米到微米厚的水层。在简化润滑近似下，排水时间在间隙 \(h\) 变小时快速增加：

\[t_{\mathrm{drain}}\sim\frac{\mu L^2}{\Delta p\,h^2},\]

其中 \(\Delta p\) 是排水压差。该式只是尺度关系，却说明了为什么“边缘先接触并分阶段干燥”优于“快速平行贴合后猛烈加热”。一旦周边先封闭，升温可能把连续水层重排成离散鼓泡，而不是将水排走。

建议先被动排水，再进行相容的低温烘烤，最后执行经验证的真空烘烤。只有在光学检查确认气泡没有增长后，才继续升温。含易挥发硫族元素的材料、有机目标、预制聚合物和热敏接触电极需要独立热预算。

验收指标与过程记录

“膜已经在目标上”不是充分验收标准。建议记录：

  • 释放前及转移后的光学拼图，并计算完整面积良率 \(Y_A=A_{\mathrm{intact}}/A_{\mathrm{target}}\)；
  • 在给定光学分辨率下统计气泡、褶皱、裂纹和缺失面积分数；
  • 转移前后的 Raman 或光致发光峰位、线宽及面扫描统计，用于区分应变、掺杂与损伤；
  • 代表区域的 AFM 粗糙度与残留/颗粒密度；
  • 面向器件时测量电连续性、迁移率、迟滞或接触电阻；
  • 路线编号；PDMS 厚度/批次与最小弯曲半径；PMMA 牌号、溶液放置时间与旋涂程序；TRT/手柄种类与批次；层压压力和前沿方向；去离子水质量与浴液使用时间；分离耗时；落片角度/速度；释放温度、停留时间与揭除方向；目标表面处理、干燥条件和溶剂批次。

丙酮去胶不等于 PMMA 完全清除。直接表征已经观察到持续残留，某些退火还可能使其碳化。因此，清洁流程必须针对具体材料验证，而不能把固定溶剂顺序当作洁净证明。对石墨烯有效的氧化清洗可能损伤半导体过渡金属硫族化合物或电极。

安全与方法边界

本节点是工艺指南，不能替代实验室风险评估。丙酮和 IPA 易燃，必须在批准的通风橱和废液体系内使用；TRT 与其他压敏胶带必须核对实验室化学和热学限制，胶黏剂过热既会污染样品，也可能释放蒸气；热板与热衬底存在灼伤和点燃风险；氧等离子体系统同时涉及射频、真空与活性气体；切割后的蓝宝石边缘和划片工具锋利；氮气必须使用合规减压装置，且不能朝向人员或无支撑薄膜喷吹。

更换供体材料、蓝宝石取向、薄膜尺寸、PMMA 配方、目标表面化学或器件形貌时，都需要重新验证方法。遇水敏感二维材料、可溶目标衬底或开放腔体结构，应考虑干法或封装环境中的替代路线。

在知识图谱中的位置

成熟度： Engineering。PDMS 直转、PMMA+手柄和自由漂浮 PMMA 都已是芯片尺度的成熟工艺族，但大面积良率、污染与应变仍取决于材料和设备。本节点把实验路线选择与二维材料物理、环境敏感的介电屏蔽、异质结器件以及光电子器件网络连接起来。

深入推导与机理解释

三条路线共享同一个界面不等式：水驱动裂纹传播必须克服钉扎、弯曲与耗散，此后才在力学路径上分开。A 路线必须把 PDMS 曲率控制到 \(h_{\mathrm{PDMS}}/(2R)\) 低于薄膜损伤应变；B 路线需要在层合支撑刚度、手柄释放剪切和聚合物残留之间平衡；C 路线则必须保持较低捞取毛细数，以维持单一排水前沿。这一框架解释了为什么单边优先、低加速度的动作适用于三条路线，而精确角度、速度和热处理配方仍需按设备标定。
